# Simple Decipher Integration Guide

This guide shows you how to add bot detection to your Decipher survey. The easy way needs no code: you upload the survey XML on the dashboard and get it back with tracking added. A copy-paste JavaScript method is described further down for cases where you cannot use the easy way.

## What This Does

Our system watches how people answer your survey (typing, mouse movements, scrolling, how open ends are entered) and checks the quality of their open-ended answers to decide whether they are real people or automated bots. It also checks for fraud signals such as duplicate respondents and data-centre IP addresses. Results show up on the dashboard while the survey is in field, and in your Decipher data.

## The Easy Way: Add Tracking on the Dashboard (Recommended)

### What you'll need
- The survey XML from Decipher (open the survey's XML editor and copy or download the whole file)
- The dashboard: https://storage.googleapis.com/bot-detection-frontend-20251208/index.html#/tracking
- About 5 minutes

### Steps
1. **Get the XML.** In Decipher, open your survey's XML editor and save the whole file as a `.xml` file (a `.txt` file holding the XML also works, up to 5 MB). It must be the complete survey, starting with `<survey ...>`, not just part of it.
2. **Open Add tracking** on the dashboard (left menu: Set up > Add tracking) and choose the file, or drop it on the page.
3. **Survey id (optional).** Leave it empty to use the survey title. This is the name your results are grouped under on the dashboard.
4. **Choose options:**
   - **Show a privacy notice on the first page** is on by default. It tells respondents the survey checks how answers are entered. You can leave the default wording or write your own.
   - **End the survey on a clear bot signal** is off by default. If you turn it on, pick the page after which the check runs and which signals end the survey. A score alone never ends the survey; only clear signals do. Pick a page every respondent reaches (not inside a loop or a conditional block). There are seven signals, all ticked by default:
     - **Automated or headless browser:** the browser says it is run by automation software.
     - **Clicks and key presses generated by script:** the browser marks at least half of the clicks and key presses as not made by a person.
     - **Pages answered with no mouse or keyboard use:** on a computer, pages were answered without moving the mouse or pressing a key.
     - **Hidden honeypot field filled in:** a box people cannot see, labelled "Leave this empty", was filled in. It never reaches your Decipher data.
     - **Open ends that appeared without typing:** an open-ended answer showed up without any typing, pasting or dictation.
     - **Typing or mouse movement with machine-exact timing:** key presses spaced almost perfectly evenly (within 3 thousandths of a second) again and again, or mouse movement in dead-straight, perfectly even steps. People are never that regular.
     - **Page times longer than the time the server saw:** the page times the browser reports add up to far more than the time our server actually saw the respondent for. Real page times can't be longer than that, so they were made up.
5. **Click Add tracking.** The page shows what was added and any warnings.
6. **Send it to Decipher.** Either:
   - click **Upload to Decipher** and choose **Create a new Decipher survey**, **Update the survey uploaded last time**, or **Replace the XML of an existing survey** (paste its path or link). Decipher checks the file first and never replaces the XML of a live survey. This button appears once an admin has saved a Decipher API key on the Settings page; or
   - click **Download**, then in Decipher open the survey's XML editor, replace the XML with the downloaded file, and save.
7. **Test it.** Take the survey yourself. Your session appears on the dashboard within a few seconds.

To change the options later, upload the tracked file again. The old tracking is replaced, not added twice.

### What gets added to your survey
- A small tracking script that runs alongside your survey's own scripts
- Decipher's `uuid` as the respondent id, so every result matches a record in your Decipher data
- A final page of hidden questions named `QOS_...` (for example `QOS_FLAG`, `QOS_SCORE`, `QOS_RISK`, `QOS_REASON`) that hold each respondent's result. Respondents never see them.
- The privacy notice (`QOS_privacy`), if you chose it
- The early end on a clear bot signal (`QOS_EARLY`, `QOS_bot_term`), if you chose it

Nothing else in your survey changes.

### What the `QOS_` values tell you
The values follow the survey's quality rules on Live Quality (the bot threshold, which bot signals count, what gets flagged, and the seconds-per-page speeder rule), so your Decipher data and the dashboard agree. A blank means that check had nothing to look at.

- `QOS_STATUS`: 1 human, 2 suspect, 3 bot. Bot or human is decided by how the person used the page; open ends and fraud have their own columns.
- `QOS_BEHAV`, `QOS_FRAUD`, `QOS_IP_RISK`, `QOS_DEVICE_RISK`, `QOS_GEO_RISK`, `QOS_VELOCITY`: risks from 0 to 100 (higher is worse).
- `QOS_OES`: open-end quality from 0 to 100 (higher is better).
- `QOS_DUP_SCORE`: duplicate risk from 0 to 100, the highest of the device reuse, IP reuse and copied-answer risks.
- `QOS_SPEEDER`: pages answered too fast, that is, the number of pages answered in less time than the survey's seconds-per-page rule.
- `QOS_STRAIGHTLINE`: grid questions straight-lined (the same answer on every row of a grid with at least 3 rows).
- `QOS_REASON` and `QOS_REASONS`: the main reason code and all of them. 1 automated browser, 2 script-generated clicks and key presses, 3 no mouse or keyboard use, 4 honeypot, 5 open ends that appeared without typing, 6 bot-like behaviour score, 7 machine-exact timing, 8 page times longer than the time the server saw, 10 data centre, VPN or proxy IP, 11 same device on other sessions, 12 same IP used repeatedly, 13 answers copied from another respondent, 14 many surveys started in a short time, 15 same respondent seen from different countries within an hour, 20 low-quality open ends, 21 straight-lining, 22 questions answered too fast. Bot signals the survey's rules switch off are never used as reasons.

**Open ends that could not be checked:** if the AI check is unavailable or too busy, an answer waits up to 20 seconds for its turn. If it still can't be checked, it is marked "not checked": it gets no score, isn't flagged, and isn't counted as a good answer. The Live Quality CSV export shows how many each respondent has (column `text_unchecked`).

**Location check:** reason 15 needs an IP-to-country lookup the service doesn't have yet, so it is left out of the fraud score for now.

### Signed tracking
If an admin has set up signing (`TRACKING_TOKEN_SECRET`), the Add tracking page says the file is signed, and the tracker carries a token for your survey. Once you upload a signed file with **Upload to Decipher** (or the first signed respondent arrives), the survey only accepts signed sessions, so fake sessions sent from outside are refused. Just downloading the file doesn't switch this on, because the live survey may still be running an older tracker. Anyone who opens the survey page can see the token, so it keeps out outsiders, not people taking the survey.

### If Decipher refuses the file
The dashboard checks for the common problems first. You'll see a warning starting with "Decipher will refuse this file:", and Upload to Decipher stops with "nothing was sent to Decipher", so no empty survey is created.

- **No `compat` setting, or `compat` below 143:** Decipher no longer accepts these. Export the XML from Decipher again (it always includes a current compat), or update the survey's compat in Decipher first.
- **`compat` 151 or higher without `delphi="1"`:** Decipher requires `delphi="1"` on the `<survey>` tag. Export from Decipher again, or add it.
- **Only part of a survey:** files without the `<survey>` tag are refused. Export the whole survey XML.
- **The survey uses quota sheets, data files (`File("x.dat")`) or resources (`${res.X}`):** those files are kept in the original Decipher survey, so a brand-new survey doesn't have them. Upload into the original survey (or a copy of it) with **Replace the XML of an existing survey**.

Any other message from Decipher, including script errors such as `Exec (page N): ...`, is shown in full so you can fix it in the survey.

### Try it first with the example survey
The file `backend/tests/fixtures/coffee_demo_survey.xml` in this project is a short coffee survey (a screener, a rating grid and two open ends) that Decipher accepts. Add tracking to it, upload it as a new survey, take it as a respondent, and check the results on Live Quality and in Decipher's data download.

### Where to see the results
- **Live Quality** on the dashboard (https://storage.googleapis.com/bot-detection-frontend-20251208/index.html#/quality): bots and quality flags for each survey, per respondent, while the survey is in field. You can adjust the rules for each survey and export a CSV.
- **Your Decipher data export:** the `QOS_` columns for each respondent.
- **Your own data file:** on Live Quality, **Add flags to a data file** takes a `.sav`, `.csv` or `.xlsx` file and gives it back with `LQ_` variables (verdict, scores and one 0/1 column per flag) added for each matched respondent. If one `uuid` has several sessions, the checked session is used first, then the latest one, rather than an abandoned earlier one.

Use this only if you cannot use the easy way. You add three pieces of JavaScript and some hidden fields by hand, and the results go into those hidden fields (not the `QOS_` variables).

### Quick Start for Testing (TL;DR)

1. **Create a test survey** with 2-3 questions
2. **Copy the code** from Step 1 into your survey header
3. **Create hidden fields** with IDs: `bot_session_id`, `bot_result`, `bot_is_bot`, `bot_confidence`
4. **Copy the code** from Step 3 into your final page
5. **Test your survey** - check the data export for bot detection results

The full steps below explain everything in detail. The open-ended answer check uses OpenAI's model (set by `OPENAI_MODEL` on the service).

### What the copy-paste method covers

| Component | Covered? | How |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| **Behavioral** (keystrokes, mouse, scroll, focus) | Yes | Step 1: events sent to `POST .../events` automatically |
| **Text quality** (open-ended questions) | Yes | Step 1: questions captured on focus, responses analyzed on blur via `POST .../text-analysis/questions` and `.../responses` |
| **Grid/Matrix** (straight-lining, patterns) | Yes (optional) | Step 1f: register grid with `question_type: 'grid'`, then send one response per cell |
| **Timing** (speeders, flatliners, per-question time) | Yes | Response time is sent with every open-ended response and with grid responses; the backend uses it for timing analysis |
| **Fraud** (IP, device, duplicates, velocity) | Yes (automatic) | No extra steps: the backend uses session + events when you call composite-analyze (Step 3) |

All of the above are combined in the **composite score** when you run the analysis at the end of the survey (Step 3). For other question types (e.g. multiple choice), you can optionally register the question with `POST /text-analysis/questions` (e.g. `question_type: 'multiple_choice'`) and send one `POST /text-analysis/responses` with `response_time_ms` when the user answers, so that question is included in timing analysis.

## Step 1: Add the Tracking Script (Once at the Beginning)

### 📍 Where to put this code:
**In Decipher:** Go to your survey editor → Survey Options → JavaScript → Header section
**Alternative:** Add to your first question as custom JavaScript

### 🎯 What it does:
- Creates a unique session ID for each user
- Starts collecting data about user behavior (typing, mouse movements, scrolling)
- Automatically detects text input fields and captures questions
- Sends data to our analysis server automatically
- Analyzes text responses in real-time using AI

### 📋 Step-by-step instructions:
1. **Open your Decipher survey** in the survey editor
2. **Click "Survey Options"** in the left sidebar
3. **Click "JavaScript"** in the options menu
4. **Click the "Header" tab** (or "Footer" if you prefer)
5. **Copy and paste the code below** into the text area
6. **Click "Save"** to save your survey

```javascript
// Bot Detection Setup with Text Quality Analysis - Add this once at the start of your survey
const API_BASE = 'https://bot-backend-119522247395.northamerica-northeast2.run.app/api/v1';

// Read identifiers (create these hidden fields in Step 2)
function getIdentifierValue(id) {
  const el = document.getElementById(id);
  return el && typeof el.value === 'string' ? el.value : '';
}

// Step 1a: Create a session when survey starts
async function setupBotDetection() {
  try {
    // Get survey identifiers for hierarchical tracking
    const surveyId = getIdentifierValue('survey_id') || '';
    const respondentId = getIdentifierValue('respondent_id') || '';
    const platformId = 'decipher'; // Platform identifier for hierarchical API
    
    // Create session with survey metadata (recommended for hierarchical tracking)
    const sessionUrl = surveyId && respondentId 
      ? `${API_BASE}/detection/sessions?survey_id=${encodeURIComponent(surveyId)}&respondent_id=${encodeURIComponent(respondentId)}&platform_id=${encodeURIComponent(platformId)}`
      : `${API_BASE}/detection/sessions`;
    
    const response = await fetch(sessionUrl, { 
      method: 'POST' 
    });
    const data = await response.json();
    
    // Store session ID in a hidden field (create this field in Decipher)
    const sessionField = document.getElementById('bot_session_id');
    if (sessionField) {
      sessionField.value = data.session_id;
    }
    
    // Ensure platform_id is consistently available for event payloads
    // (so events match the same platform used during session creation)
    const platformField = document.getElementById('platform_id');
    if (platformField) {
      platformField.value = platformId;
    }

    // Capture device/page metadata once at start for convenience
    const pageUrl = window.location.href;
    const userAgent = navigator.userAgent;
    const screenWidth = window.screen.width;
    const screenHeight = window.screen.height;
    const viewportWidth = window.innerWidth;
    const viewportHeight = window.innerHeight;

    // Optionally store for export visibility (requires fields created in Step 2)
    const pageUrlField = document.getElementById('page_url');
    if (pageUrlField) pageUrlField.value = pageUrl;
    const userAgentField = document.getElementById('user_agent');
    if (userAgentField) userAgentField.value = userAgent;

    // Prime first metadata event so backend has device context immediately
    window.eventBuffer.push({
      event_type: 'session_start',
      timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
      page_url: pageUrl,
      user_agent: userAgent,
      screen_width: screenWidth,
      screen_height: screenHeight,
      viewport_width: viewportWidth,
      viewport_height: viewportHeight,
      survey_id: getIdentifierValue('survey_id'),
      respondent_id: getIdentifierValue('respondent_id'),
      platform_id: platformId
    });
  } catch (error) {
    console.log('Bot detection setup failed:', error);
  }
}

// Step 1b: Text Quality Analysis Setup
let currentQuestionId = null;
let questionStartTime = null;

// Find question text near an input element
function findQuestionText(inputElement) {
  if (!inputElement) return '';
  
  // Look for nearby text elements that could be the question
  const parent = inputElement.closest('div, p, li, td, th, label');
  if (parent) {
    const textNodes = Array.from(parent.childNodes).filter(node => 
      node.nodeType === Node.TEXT_NODE && node.textContent.trim().length > 10
    );
    if (textNodes.length > 0) {
      return textNodes[0].textContent.trim();
    }
    
    // Look for labels or other text elements
    const labels = parent.querySelectorAll('label, span, p, div');
    for (let label of labels) {
      if (label.textContent.trim().length > 10 && !label.querySelector('input, textarea')) {
        return label.textContent.trim();
      }
    }
  }
  
  return '';
}

// Capture a survey question
async function captureQuestion(inputElement) {
  const sessionId = document.getElementById('bot_session_id')?.value;
  if (!sessionId) return;
  
  const questionText = findQuestionText(inputElement);
  if (!questionText) return;
  
  try {
    const response = await fetch(`${API_BASE}/text-analysis/questions`, {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        session_id: sessionId,
        question_text: questionText,
        question_type: 'open_ended',
        element_id: inputElement.id || inputElement.name,
        page_url: window.location.href,
        page_title: document.title
      })
    });
    
    const data = await response.json();
    currentQuestionId = data.question_id;
    questionStartTime = Date.now();
  } catch (error) {
    console.log('Question capture failed:', error);
  }
}

// Analyze a response
async function analyzeResponse(inputElement, responseText) {
  const sessionId = document.getElementById('bot_session_id')?.value;
  if (!sessionId || !currentQuestionId || !responseText || responseText.length < 10) return;
  
  const responseTime = Date.now() - questionStartTime;
  
  try {
    const response = await fetch(`${API_BASE}/text-analysis/responses`, {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        session_id: sessionId,
        question_id: currentQuestionId,
        response_text: responseText,
        response_time_ms: responseTime
      })
    });
    
    const data = await response.json();
    
    // Store text quality results in hidden fields
    const qualityField = document.getElementById('text_quality_score');
    if (qualityField) qualityField.value = data.quality_score || '';
    
    const flaggedField = document.getElementById('text_flagged');
    if (flaggedField) flaggedField.value = data.is_flagged ? 'true' : 'false';
    
  } catch (error) {
    console.log('Response analysis failed:', error);
  }
}

// Step 1c: Collect and send user behavior data
window.eventBuffer = window.eventBuffer || [];
const BATCH_SIZE = 10;

function baseContext(e) {
  return {
    page_url: window.location.href,
    user_agent: navigator.userAgent,
    screen_width: window.screen.width,
    screen_height: window.screen.height,
    viewport_width: window.innerWidth,
    viewport_height: window.innerHeight,
    survey_id: getIdentifierValue('survey_id'),
    respondent_id: getIdentifierValue('respondent_id'),
    platform_id: getIdentifierValue('platform_id'),
    element_id: e && e.target && e.target.id ? e.target.id : undefined,
    element_type: e && e.target && e.target.tagName ? e.target.tagName.toLowerCase() : undefined
  };
}

function sendEvents() {
  const sessionId = document.getElementById('bot_session_id')?.value;
  if (!sessionId || window.eventBuffer.length === 0) return;
  
  fetch(`${API_BASE}/detection/sessions/${sessionId}/events`, {
    method: 'POST',
    headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
    body: JSON.stringify(window.eventBuffer)
  }).then(() => {
    window.eventBuffer = []; // Clear buffer after sending
  }).catch(() => {
    // If sending fails, keep events for next attempt
  });
}

// Step 1d: Track user interactions
document.addEventListener('keydown', (e) => {
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'keystroke',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    key: e.key,
    ...baseContext(e)
  });
  
  if (window.eventBuffer.length >= BATCH_SIZE) {
    sendEvents();
  }
});

document.addEventListener('click', (e) => {
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'click',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    button: e.button,
    ...baseContext(e)
  });
});

let lastMouseEventAt = 0;
document.addEventListener('mousemove', (e) => {
  const now = Date.now();
  if (now - lastMouseEventAt < 50) return; // simple throttle ~20fps
  lastMouseEventAt = now;
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'mouse_move',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    x: e.clientX,
    y: e.clientY,
    ...baseContext(e)
  });
});

document.addEventListener('scroll', () => {
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'scroll',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    scroll_y: window.scrollY,
    scroll_x: window.scrollX,
    page_height: document.documentElement.scrollHeight,
    page_width: document.documentElement.scrollWidth,
    ...baseContext()
  });
});

// Step 1e: Text input tracking for quality analysis
document.addEventListener('focusin', (e) => {
  const target = e.target;
  if (target.tagName === 'TEXTAREA' || (target.tagName === 'INPUT' && target.type === 'text')) {
    captureQuestion(target);
  }
  
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'focus',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    ...baseContext(e)
  });
});

document.addEventListener('focusout', (e) => {
  const target = e.target;
  if (target.tagName === 'TEXTAREA' || (target.tagName === 'INPUT' && target.type === 'text')) {
    analyzeResponse(target, target.value);
  }
  
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'blur',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    ...baseContext(e)
  });
});

document.addEventListener('visibilitychange', () => {
  window.eventBuffer.push({
    event_type: 'visibility_change',
    timestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    visibility_state: document.visibilityState,
    ...baseContext()
  });
});

// Send any remaining events every 5 seconds
setInterval(sendEvents, 5000);

// Start everything when page loads
setupBotDetection();
```

### Step 1f: Grid/Matrix Questions (Optional)

If your survey has **grid** or **matrix** questions (rows of items with the same scale), you can capture them so the system can analyze straight-lining and response patterns.

**Two steps:**

1. **Register the grid when it is shown**  
   Call the questions API once with `question_type: 'grid'` (or `'matrix'`) and store the returned `question_id` (e.g. in a variable keyed by your Decipher question ID).

2. **When the respondent has completed the grid** (e.g. on "Next" or when leaving the page), collect each selected value (one per cell) and send **one** `POST /text-analysis/responses` **per cell** with that same `question_id` and `response_text` = the cell value (e.g. `"1"`, `"5"`).

**Register the grid question** (run when the grid page loads or when the grid becomes visible):

```javascript
// Call when the grid question is shown (e.g. on page load of the page with the grid)
async function registerGridQuestion(questionText, elementId) {
  const sessionId = document.getElementById('bot_session_id')?.value;
  if (!sessionId) return null;
  try {
    const response = await fetch(`${API_BASE}/text-analysis/questions`, {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        session_id: sessionId,
        question_text: questionText || 'Grid question',
        question_type: 'grid',
        element_id: elementId || '',
        page_url: window.location.href,
        page_title: document.title
      })
    });
    const data = await response.json();
    return data.question_id;
  } catch (e) {
    console.log('Grid question registration failed:', e);
    return null;
  }
}
```

**Send grid cell values** (call when the respondent leaves the grid or clicks Next; pass the `question_id` from above and an array of selected values, one per cell, in order):

```javascript
// Call after grid is completed; cellValues = array of selected values, e.g. ['1','2','5','4','3']
async function sendGridResponses(questionId, responseTimeMs, cellValues) {
  const sessionId = document.getElementById('bot_session_id')?.value;
  if (!sessionId || !questionId || !cellValues || !cellValues.length) return;
  for (const value of cellValues) {
    try {
      await fetch(`${API_BASE}/text-analysis/responses`, {
        method: 'POST',
        headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
        body: JSON.stringify({
          session_id: sessionId,
          question_id: questionId,
          response_text: String(value),
          response_time_ms: responseTimeMs || null
        })
      });
    } catch (e) {
      console.log('Grid response send failed:', e);
    }
  }
}
```

**How to get `cellValues` in Decipher:**  
If the grid is rendered as HTML (e.g. table with inputs or radio groups), when the respondent moves to the next page (or on a "Next" button click), loop through the grid inputs and collect the selected value for each cell (e.g. `input.value` or the checked radio's value), in a consistent order (e.g. row by row). Pass that array as `cellValues`. If Decipher exposes a JavaScript API for question values (e.g. `getValue()` for the grid), use that to build the array of cell values instead.

**Note:** The backend uses one `survey_response` per cell. Grid analysis (straight-lining, patterns, variance) runs when you call the composite-analyze endpoint at the end of the survey (Step 3).

**Also create this hidden field in Decipher:**
- Field ID: `bot_session_id`
- Field Type: Hidden/Text
- This stores the session ID for tracking

```javascript
// Bot Detection Analysis with Text Quality - Add this at the end of your survey
async function analyzeBotDetection() {
  const sessionId = document.getElementById('bot_session_id')?.value;
  if (!sessionId) return;
  
  try {
    // Send any remaining events before analysis
    const remainingEvents = (window.eventBuffer || []).length;
    if (remainingEvents > 0) {
      await fetch(`${API_BASE}/detection/sessions/${sessionId}/events`, {
        method: 'POST',
        headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
        body: JSON.stringify(window.eventBuffer)
      });
      window.eventBuffer = [];
    }
    
    // Run composite analysis (behavioral + text quality)
    const response = await fetch(`${API_BASE}/detection/sessions/${sessionId}/composite-analyze`, {
      method: 'POST'
    });
    const result = await response.json();
    
    // Save results to hidden fields
    const resultField = document.getElementById('bot_result');
    if (resultField) resultField.value = JSON.stringify(result);
    const isBotField = document.getElementById('bot_is_bot');
    if (isBotField) isBotField.value = result.is_bot ? 'true' : 'false';
    const confidenceField = document.getElementById('bot_confidence');
    if (confidenceField) confidenceField.value = String(result.composite_score ?? result.confidence_score ?? '0');
    
    // Update text quality fields with latest results
    const qualityField = document.getElementById('text_quality_score');
    if (qualityField && result.text_quality_score) {
      qualityField.value = result.text_quality_score;
    }
    
    const flaggedField = document.getElementById('text_flagged');
    if (flaggedField && result.text_quality_details) {
      const isFlagged = result.text_quality_details.flagged_count > 0;
      flaggedField.value = isFlagged ? 'true' : 'false';
    }
    
  } catch (error) {
    console.log('Bot analysis failed:', error);
    // Set default values if analysis fails
    const resultField = document.getElementById('bot_result');
    if (resultField) resultField.value = '{"error": "analysis_failed"}';
    const isBotField = document.getElementById('bot_is_bot');
    if (isBotField) isBotField.value = 'unknown';
    const confidenceField = document.getElementById('bot_confidence');
    if (confidenceField) confidenceField.value = '0';
  }
}

// Run analysis when survey is submitted
analyzeBotDetection();
```

## How It Works (Simple Explanation)

1. **Session Creation:** When someone starts your survey, we create a unique session ID to track them.

2. **Behavioral Data Collection:** Our script automatically watches for:
   - **Keystrokes:** What keys they press and how fast
   - **Mouse movements:** How they move their mouse around
   - **Scrolling:** How they scroll through pages
   - **Focus changes:** When they click on different parts of the page

3. **Text Quality Collection:** When users type in text fields, we also:
   - **Capture questions:** Automatically find and save the question text
   - **Analyze responses:** Use AI to check if responses are gibberish, copy-pasted, irrelevant, or generic
   - **Score quality:** Give each response a quality score from 0-100
   - **Record response time:** Time to answer is sent with each response and used for timing analysis (speeders, flatliners)

4. **Data Sending:** Every 10 events or every 5 seconds, we send the collected data to our analysis server.

5. **Real-time Analysis:** As users finish typing, we immediately analyze their text responses using OpenAI's AI model.

6. **Final Analysis:** When the survey is complete, we run **composite analysis** (behavioral + text quality + fraud + grid + timing) to determine if it looks like human or bot behavior.

7. **Results:** The final result is saved in your hidden fields:
   - `bot_is_bot`: "true" if detected as bot, "false" if human (decided by behaviour, using the survey's quality rules)
   - `bot_confidence`: A number from 0-1 (composite score: behavioral + text quality + fraud)
   - `bot_result`: Full analysis details including behavioral, text quality, fraud, grid, and timing
   - `text_quality_score`: Average quality score 0-100 for all text responses
   - `text_flagged`: "true" if any text response was flagged as low quality
